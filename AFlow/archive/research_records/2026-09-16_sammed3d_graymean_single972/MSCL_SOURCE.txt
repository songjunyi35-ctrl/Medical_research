Abstract. Precise segmentation of postoperative glioma poses a piv-
otal task in medical image processing due to the crucialness of assessing
treatment protocols and directing further therapeutic measures. Despite
numerous studies on primary tumors and the existence of highly effective
models, research on residual tumors is relatively scarce due to difficul-
ties in annotation and sample acquisition. Since preoperative glioma are
tumorsbeforesurgicalresectionofpostoperativeglioma,providingsignif-
icant assistance in the segmentation of gliomas postsurgery, we propose
a curriculum learning approach for postoperative glioma segmentation.
Instead of directly segmenting postoperative glioma, our method estab-
lishes a multistage curriculum system based on the ease of data, enabling
the model to engage in continuous learning from preoperative to post-
operative data, which preserves the dark knowledge acquired in previous
tasks. We train the public dataset of primary tumors at the first stage
for easy data learning and postoperative glioma public dataset at the
second stage for medium data learning, ultimately, we undertake target
domain adaptation for background knowledge learning at the last stage
for hard data learning. We evaluated the proposed method on three
public postoperative glioma datasets and one private dataset, achiev-
ing improvements in DSC by 11.7%, 15.1%, 8.2%, and 14.2% compared
to fine-tuning foundation model directly; 11.4%, 7.8%, 14.5% and 8.7%
compared to state-of-the-art methods.

1 Introduction
Glioma represents the most prevalent type of primary intracranial tumor, stem-
ming from the malignant transformation of glial cells in the brain and spinal
cord. Surgical resection is the primary choice of treatment for glioma. However,
due to the location and invasive nature of the tumour, complete resection can
be challenging, mainly when the tumour is situated in functional areas of the
brain. Therefore, doctors should develop optimal treatment strategies based on
the patient’s post-operative condition to effectively control tumour growth and
2 Anonymized Author et al.
improve prognosis. It underscores the importance of accurately identifying resid-
ual tumours after surgery. Nevertheless, the identification of residual tumours
is a very challenging task. Because of the inherent infiltrative growth pattern
of gliomas, there is no clear boundary between the residual tumor and adjacent
tissues, and the tumor tissues become fragmented after further surgery, all of
which pose a great challenge in the segmentation of the residual tumor.
Toeffectivelyaddressthechallengesposedbythesecomplexanddiversedata,
Curriculum Learning, as a gradual learning strategy, is adopted in our method.
In this paper, we establish a multi-stage curriculum system based on the com-
plexity of the tumor, enabling the model to progressively learn from simple to
complex knowledge and to continuously learn from preoperative to postopera-
tive data, thereby retaining the tacit knowledge acquired from previous tasks.
In the first stage, we incorporate publicly available datasets of smaller primary
tumors into the training set, allowing the model to acquire the ability to segment
primary tumors. In the second stage, we introduce datasets of residual tumors
with gold standards, enabling the model to learn a certain level of ability to
segment residual tumors. However, due to differences in background knowledge
between public datasets and the target domain, applying the model from this
stagetosegmentresidualtumorsinthetargetdomainmaynotyieldgoodresults.
Therefore, we incorporate a third stage into the curriculum for target domain
adaptation focused on background knowledge learning. Here we propose data
simulation approaches for residual tumour segmentation, utilizing the preopera-
tive dataset to simulate surgical resection and generate simulated postoperative
residual tumours adding into the thrid stage training set. This method obtains
precise boundaries and gold-standard masks of simulated post-operative tumors.
For summarization, our major contributions are:
1) We have established a multi-stage curriculum system based on the com-
plexity of tumors, enabling the model to progressively learn from simple to
complex knowledge. It continuously learns from preoperative to postoperative
data, ultimately achieving excellent segmentation performance for postoperative
gliomas in the target domain.
2) We have proposed a data simulation model that utilizes the preoperative
dataset to simulate resection operations, generating simulated diverse-volume
postoperative gliomas for target domain background adaptation.
3) We have designed a fine-tuning strategy that is combined with the opti-
mization of prompt points in the last stage, which integrates a novel loss function
that incorporates the MSE loss between the grayscale threshold of the predicted
tumor region, calculated through hint points, and the gray-scale threshold of the
actual tumor region into the loss of segmentation training. This integration aims
to optimize the prompt points and achieve precise segmentation.

2.1 Multi-Stage Curriculum System
Our objective is to achieve precise segmentation of rare and difficult-to-segment
post-operativegliomas.Mainstreamapproaches,suchasfine-tuningusingdatasets
with gold standard annotations, have been employed; however, these methods
struggle to adapt to the features of target domains sourced from multiple cen-
ters, leading to catastrophic forgetting of accumulated knowledge. By designing
a learning curriculum, we can assist the model in gradually establishing an un-
derstanding of image features, thereby enhancing its generalization ability and
recognition accuracy, mitigating overfitting issues, and strengthening the model’s
adaptability to unseen data.
In this paper, we have designed a multi-stage curriculum based on the frame-
work of Difficulty Measurer + Training Scheduler, and incorporated the difficulty
4 Anonymized Author et al.
measurer at different stages as needed to select data in real-time for inclusion
in the training set. The Training Scheduler employs the Baby Step Training
Scheduler, with the process outlined below. Here we choose∗ ∈ {1, 2} and k = 3
because there are two difficulty measurers and three stages in our method.
Stage 1: Easy-data Learning The first step is to engage with easy data.
Here, we select a public dataset of primary tumors, which is then processed by
a difficulty measure, denoted C1, to determine the data that will be included
in the training first phase curriculum. In this context,C1 serves as a volume
measurer, selecting primary tumors with smaller volumes to be incorporated into
the training set for the first phase. Since primary tumors are generally larger in
size and have a relatively regular and uniform shape compared to postoperative
gliomas, they pose a lower segmentation difficulty.
Stage 2: Medium-data Learning However, due to the properties of post-
operative gliomas, such as their blurred boundaries, infiltrative growth, and
varying morphologies and sizes, the first-phase model cannot perform well in
segmentation. Therefore, new data will be incorporated in the second phase.
BRATS2024 is a post-operative glioma dataset with gold standards. Here, we
use the model trained in the first phase as a difficulty measurerC2. Data from
the BRATS2024 dataset that yield a Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) of less
than 0.5 when tested through the model training from the previous stage are
selected as training data for the second phase and added to the training set for
learning.
Stage 3: Hard-data LearningAfter the second phase of training, the model
acquires the ability to segment postoperative gliomas. However, due to signifi-
cant differences between the target domain data and the public dataset in terms
of background, tumor volume, and other aspects, target domain adaptation is
necessary to equip the model with generalization capabilities in the target do-
main. Since clinically, postoperative gliomas are difficult to obtain and annotate,
there is a scarcity of data with gold standards, making fine-tuning challenging. In
contrast, primary tumors are relatively easier to access, and residual tumors are
obtained from the resection of primary tumors. Consequently, a two-stage data
simulation method is proposed for data augmentation to facilitate fine-tuning,
enabling the model to generalize within the target domain. The detailed process
will be introduced in section 2.2.
2.2 Stage 3: Background Adaptation—Data Simulation

tive growth, annotating them is very challenging and time-consuming. However,
one thing is certain: residual tumors are obtained from the resection of primary
tumors. Consequently, a two-stage data simulation method is proposed. First,

3 Experiments
3.1 Datasets and Implementation Details
Brats2022 (1261 cases) and 2024 (1350 cases) are two public datasets. RHUH
and ReMIND are two small-sample public datasets, which we use 40 and 29
cases separately. Notably, the ReMIND dataset does not include post-operative
data for all patients, and some tumors are not gliomas. Hence, the numbers
displayed in the table represent the filtered quantities after such considerations.
Moreover, the in-house dataset (60 cases) is from our local hospital.Here, we use
pre-operative data to simulate post-operative data for data simulation in the
third stage of our curriculumn learning systemand fine-tune the segment with
them. Then, post-operative data is for validation.
We used PyTorch for experiments on two Tesla v100 GPUs with an In-
tel Xeon Gold 6230 processor on Linux. Our backbone was the SAM-Med 3D
model. MRI images were preprocessed with normalization, intensity correction,
and skull stripping, then transformed and resized to 128×128×128 using Tor-
chIO. Training used a batch size of 1, learning rate of 8e-4, weight decay of 0.1,
for 200 epochs.

4 Conclusion
In this paper, we mainly address the issues of limited data and high annota-
tion costs by proposing multi-stage curriculumn learning models that enable the
model to engage in continuous learning from preoperative to postoperative data,
which preserves the dark knowledge acquired in previous tasks. In the first stage,
we choose pre-operative gliomas from pubilc datasets for easy-data-learning; in
the second stage, we choose data from the public postoperative dataset that yield
a DSC of less than 0.5 when tested through the model training from the previous
stage; Ultimately, we undertake target domain adaptation for background knowl-
edge learning, introducing a novel simulation method, which leverages few-shot
primary tumors from the target domain to emulate postoperative gliomas using
level-set method, enhancing our dataset. We evaluated the proposed method on
three public postoperative glioma datasets and one private dataset, achieving im-
provements in DSC by 11.7%, 15.1%, 8.2%, and 14.2% compared to fine-tuning
foundation model directly; 11.4%, 7.8%, 14.5% and 8.7% compared to state-of-
the-art methods. These results underscore the effectiveness and robustness of the
proposed approach in addressing the challenges associated with post-operative
glioma segmentation.
